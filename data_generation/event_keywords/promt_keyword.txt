1. Analyze the conversation between the patient and chat doctor.
2. Generate keywords based on the conversation.
3. Extract a list of medically and conversationally relevant keywords or short phrases from the conversation. 
Keywords may include symptoms, conditions, medications, tests, concerns, or clinical actions.
Avoid generic words (e.g., “the”, “and”, “okay”).
4. For each extracted keyword, assign a score from 1 to 10 indicating how commonly that keyword appears in real-world doctor–patient conversations, where:
1 = Rarely used in clinical doctor–patient interactions
10 = Very frequently used and clinically standard
The ranking should reflect real clinical conversational frequency, not just how often the word appears in this single dialogue.
5. Do not hallucinate diagnoses beyond what is stated or clearly implied.
6. Use neutral, professional clinical language.
7. Arrange the extracted keywords in descending order of their assigned rank, such that: 
Keywords with a rank of 10 appear first.
Keywords with lower ranks follow in decreasing order (9 → 1).
Keywords with the same rank may appear in any order relative to each other.
8. Generate 500 keywords.
